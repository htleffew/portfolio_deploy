# Profile: MEDIUM (standalone public essays)

Applies to Medium articles and standalone public essays, published through
`Pm_html/portfolio_engine/deployment_engine/medium-cli/` (articles live as
`articles/<slug>/index.md`). Obey **CORE** first, then everything here.

Medium shares the portfolio voice but in an essay register for a general technical
reader who did not arrive from the portfolio site and has no surrounding context.

## How Medium differs from the portfolio profile

- **First-person narrative lead is encouraged.** The strongest prior example is
  "Gaslighting in the Name of AI Safety" (Leffew, Oct 2025): it opens on the seed
  encounter, then generalizes. Start from the concrete experience or finding, then
  widen to the claim.
- **Self-contained.** Define terms and stakes in the piece; do not assume the
  reader has seen the portfolio, the dataset, or prior articles. A one-line "what
  this is and why I looked" near the top is fine; throat-clearing about why the
  topic matters is not.
- **Essayistic cadence, CORE restraint.** Longer arcs and personal framing are
  allowed, but every CORE rule still holds: no manufactured drama, no canned
  openers, no banned vocabulary, no dashes.

## The proprietary boundary still applies

The same research-vs-employer line as the portfolio profile governs Medium:

- Heather's **own independent research** is hers to publish — be specific, name the
  numbers and methods.
- Anything from a **professional position** is taught as a generalizable method with
  no employer specifics and no proprietary metrics.

## Medium mechanics

- Title and subtitle carry the piece; make the title concrete, not a teaser.
- One clear thesis per article; the narrative serves it.
- Attribute quotes and data; link to the primary source or the portfolio page when
  the work is hers.

## Exit checklist

- [ ] CORE clean
- [ ] Opens on a concrete encounter or finding, not a thesis statement
- [ ] Self-contained: a cold reader understands it without the portfolio
- [ ] Proprietary boundary respected
- [ ] Title is concrete; one thesis; sources attributed
