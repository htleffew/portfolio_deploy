## Two burdens.

Anyone who makes a claim about how an artificial system behaves takes on two burdens at once, and the same evidence seldom meets both. The first burden is existence at scale, meaning the behavior occurs across many interactions at a rate you can bound, beyond the single transcript someone shared online; the second burden is identity, meaning the behavior has a definite structure, an anatomy that separates it from the behaviors it resembles. Corpus-level evidence is informative about the first burden and nearly uninformative about the second; case-level evidence is informative about the second and nearly uninformative about the first; a study of a deployed model needs both tiers, because a finding built on one tier alone is either a number with no referent or a story with no warrant to generalize. When I characterized the Long Conversation Reminder behavior in Claude Sonnet 4.5, the finding rested on two numbers that do different jobs, 26,158 corpus rows and 35 hand-coded cases, and that analysis is the worked instance below.

At the corpus tier, you count; you aggregate observations over a population, report how often a marker appears, and make statements about prevalence. At the case tier, you read; you examine individual instances closely enough to say what the behavior is when it occurs, what its internal structure looks like, and how it differs from the adjacent behaviors it resembles on the surface. A behavioral claim about a model is valid at the join of the two tiers, and a researcher who keeps the tiers distinct, in the analysis and in any later replication, keeps the claim within its actual reach.

<Band theme="dark" spine="Base rates without structure" section="1. Base rates without structure.">

## 1. Base rates without structure.

A frequency count over a corpus is a statement that a token or a marker appears some number of times; whether the counted instances share a structure, or are even the same behavior, is a separate question, and the count is silent on it. The count is an extensional fact about strings; the behavior is an intensional fact about what the model did; the gap between the two is the usual source of error in a raw corpus measure, and two failures account for most of it.

Polysemy is the first failure. In the Long Conversation Reminder corpus, users write "professional" mostly in work senses (their own professional identity, a professional setting, output that looks professional), while the system-prompt text and the model's directives use it in the licensed-clinician sense ("speaks with a professional or trusted person for support"); a researcher who counts both senses together has measured neither. Sense discovery is the remedy, and a workable version is HDBSCAN clustering (McInnes et al., 2017) over sentence embeddings of keyword-in-context windows, which separates the clinical sense from the work senses before you trust any rate; without that step, a remark about work quality and a directive about the user's mental state count as one thing.

Voice contamination is the second failure. In a single social-media comment, a user can interleave their own narration, verbatim model output, paraphrase, and sometimes the literal system-prompt text, and from the surface string alone nobody can tell which voice produced a keyword. The claim under test is about what the model said, and what users reported the model saying is a separate object, so span-level voice segmentation into four voice classes (user narration, direct quotation of the model, paraphrase of the model, and system-prompt reproduction) comes before any span counts as model output. A researcher who computes a raw keyword rate without that separation counts user complaints about the behavior as instances of it.

A deeper limit remains even after a clean count. Suppose you resolve the polysemy and the voice contamination and recover a clean rate of clinical-register language. The rate is still silent on whether the language was solicited, whether the model yielded when the user pushed back, and whether the conversation moved toward restriction or toward wider options for the user. An aggregate has no internal anatomy; it is evidence that something occurs at a frequency, and what that something is remains an open question.

</Band>

## 2. Anecdote without prevalence.

A single case read closely does the work a corpus count cannot. The case is proof that the behavior is possible, and in reading it you can lay out the internal structure of one instance, naming who initiated, what the model asserted, on what evidence, and how it responded to correction. Phenomenological reading is the origin of a construct; the categories a researcher later counts in a corpus come from looking hard at instances before there is anything to tabulate.

A case is silent on rate. From one transcript, nobody can say whether it is representative or anomalous, whether it reflects a property of the system or an accident of a single generation, or whether it would recur on a fresh prompt. A screenshot can be edited, a prompt can be omitted, and a one-off sampling artifact can pass for a disposition. The idiographic depth that makes a case informative about structure is the same depth that leaves it mute about frequency. Psychologists have drawn the line between idiographic and nomothetic knowledge for nearly a century (Allport, 1937), and the line is as relevant at the scale of model-interaction data as it was in personality research.

The characteristic error with case-only evidence is the inference from vividness to prevalence. A behavior that reads as alarming in one transcript feels common because it is memorable, and a feeling is a poor substitute for a measurement. Case-study methodologists have spent decades on the conditions under which a few instances can license a general claim; in Yin's account, a researcher generalizes from cases analytically, to a theory or a mechanism, and generalizes statistically only from a population sample (Yin, 2018). A case is evidence of what the behavior is, and a corpus is evidence of how much of it there is.

<Band theme="dark" spine="Extension and intension" section="3. Extension and intension.">

## 3. Extension and intension.

The two tiers are an instance of an old logical distinction. Extension is the set of instances a term picks out, which for a behavior means how often and how widely it occurs; intension is the set of properties that define it. The corpus tier fixes extension, bounding the rate within the retrieval frame and showing the behavior across a population of posts beyond any single user's feed. The case tier fixes intension, specifying the construct, the definite structure that makes one instance the same behavior as another. A claim of the form "the system does X" is valid when both are settled, because with extension unsettled X may be anecdotal, and with intension unsettled X may be undefined.

The dependence runs in both directions. The definition of what to count comes from the cases; in the worked instance, the five-property definition that separates a boundary violation from ordinary expressed concern came from reading instances against the boundary-violation literature, and without it a corpus count has no defined target. The question of reach goes to the corpus; a structure recovered from thirty-five instances becomes a claim about a deployed model only after the corpus shows that those instances come from a population where the behavior recurs. Each tier is the check on the other, and neither tier is a check on itself.

</Band>

<Band theme="paper" spine="The LCR in two tiers" section="4. The Long Conversation Reminder in two tiers.">

## 4. The Long Conversation Reminder in two tiers.

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 4.5 on September 29, 2025, with a system-prompt scaffold that users came to call the Long Conversation Reminder, and users soon reported the model issuing unsolicited psychiatric attributions during ordinary sessions, labeling coding work and creative writing as evidence of mania, perfectionism, or detachment from reality. The question I set out to answer was whether those reports, which circulated as screenshots and transcripts, described a real property of the deployed system or a scatter of memorable one-offs.

The corpus tier answered the extension question. I built a pipeline over 26,158 Reddit rows from four subreddits, spanning August 1 through December 31, 2025, and opened the window about two months before the release so the pre-release baseline could be measured directly (8,394 rows). Voice segmentation separated model output from user narration, HDBSCAN sense discovery separated the clinical sense of "professional" from the work senses, and the cleaned corpus served as the frame around the cases.

The case tier answered the identity question. I hand-coded 35 purposively selected cases against a five-property definition drawn from the boundary-violation literature (Gutheil & Gabbard, 1993; Leffew, 2026). The five properties are unsolicited issuance, a weak inferential signal as the only grounds for the directive, refusal to yield under pushback, movement toward restriction of the user's options, and persistence across fresh sessions. Three of the five properties were uniform. All 35 directives were unsolicited, all 35 moved toward restriction, and in the 5 cases where a user pushed back, the model escalated all 5 times and yielded in none. The other two properties varied; 12 of the 35 cases showed cross-session persistence, and 21 of the 35 had an identifiable weak signal.

The finding is the conjunction of the two tiers. On their own, thirty-five cases are a small set of anecdotes with no warrant to generalize, and a raw count of keyword hits is a contaminated number with no anatomy. In the corpus, users described the behavior across four communities; in the cases, the same three-property structure appeared every time the directive fired, and a search of all 26,158 rows for deviant cases recovered a single yield. Within the hand-coded sample, a behavior with no variance in direction or in response to correction is consistent with a trigger in the system prompt, and that reading was available only because one tier supplied breadth and the other supplied structure. The corpus method is described in [Reading Misalignment Off the Public Record](/public-discourse-misalignment/), and the full corpus and case report is [Pathologizing Without Warrant](/claude-lcr-analysis/).

<Band theme="dark" spine="Replication is two acts" section="5. Replication is two acts.">

## 5. Replication is two acts.

The two-tier structure has a consequence for replication. Replicating the corpus finding and replicating the case characterization are different acts, with different procedures and different meanings when they fail, and a reviewer who treats them as one act can draw the wrong conclusion from a partial result.

Replicating the corpus finding is a measurement-reproduction act on a population. It means collecting an independent window, running the same voice segmentation and sense disambiguation, and recovering a comparable rate in a fresh sample. It fails when the rate does not recur, and a failure of this kind is evidence that the behavior was transient or window-specific, or that the deployment changed. Anthropic's November 19, 2025 revision of the published prompt removed the escalating-detachment clause and added the sentence "Reasonable disagreements between the person and Claude should not be considered detachment from reality" (Anthropic, 2025). The rate should fall in any post-revision window, and a lower rate there reads as evidence that the revision worked; the original measurement of the pre-revision window stays correct.

Replicating the case characterization is an interpretive-reliability act on instances. It means coding the same transcripts, or fresh ones, against the five-property definition and recovering the same structural signature, ideally with a second coder and a reported agreement statistic such as Krippendorff's alpha (Krippendorff, 2019). It fails when independent coders cannot recover the signature, and a failure of this kind is evidence that the construct was misspecified or coder-dependent; it is silent on how common the behavior was. A signature that independent coders cannot recover is a problem with the definition; a rate that does not recur in a fresh window is a problem with, or a change in, the world.

A reviewer who conflates the two acts makes a category error. "I could not reproduce your thirty-five cases" and "I could not reproduce your rate" are different objections with different conclusions, and they age differently; the corpus rate is tied to a deployment window and should change when the deployment changes, while the structural signature is a claim about the behavior's anatomy whenever it occurred, and it remains true after the rate drops. A study can pass one act and fail the other; when the tiers stay separate, each failure stays legible as its own result, and a reader can weigh each one on its own.

</Band>

<Band theme="paper" spine="What a two-tier claim contains" section="What a two-tier claim contains.">

## What a two-tier claim contains.

A two-tier claim has three parts, an existence-and-rate claim from the corpus, stated as a bounded frequency; a structure-and-reliability claim from the cases, stated as a coded signature; and a general claim about the system, made where both hold. Three inferences stay out of bounds, a population rate drawn from a purposive case sample, a behavioral identity drawn from a frequency count, and a single replication standard applied to both tiers at once. A purposive case sample is no basis for a rate, a count of surface markers is no definition of a construct, and a reviewer who asks one tier to do the other's job will mistake a partial finding for a refuted one.

In the worked instance, the corpus came from seed matching on four subreddits, so its counts describe structure inside that frame; the 35 cases were chosen purposively, so their 100 percent rates are properties of that sample; one coder coded them, so a blinded second-coder pass is the natural next step; and voice could be assigned reliably for 4.12 percent of corpus characters, so the rest of the corpus reads as user narration about the behavior. Each of those is a scope statement of the kind a two-tier claim should carry on its face, since a reader who knows which tier produced which number can reject either one and keep the other.

The discipline is modest. When you make a behavioral claim about a deployed model, report the rate and the structure separately, because the evidence for each is separate. Counting a behavior tells you how often it happens; characterizing it tells you what it is; a claim about a deployed model wants both, and I hope the distinction saves you a round with a reviewer.
